The Nature of the Mind

Why is it so difficult to control our desires, anger, and attachments? Arjuna voices the exact same struggle 5,000 years ago:

चञ्चलं हि मन: कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवद्दृढम् |
तस्याहं निग्रहं मन्ये वायोरिव सुदुष्करम् ||
cañcalaṁ hi manaḥ kṛṣṇa
pramāthi balavad dṛḍham
tasyāhaṁ nigrahaṁ manye
vāyor iva su-duṣkaram
"For the mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
— Bhagavad Gītā 6.34

Our Best Friend or Worst Enemy?

All suffering and happiness originate within the mind. An uncontrolled mind leads to degradation, while a controlled mind leads to ultimate peace.

बन्धुरात्मात्मनस्तस्य येनात्मैवात्मना जित: |
अनात्मनस्तु शत्रुत्वे वर्ते तात्मैव शत्रुवत् ||
bandhur ātmātmanas tasya
yenātmaivātmanā jitaḥ
anātmanas tu śatrutve
vartetātmaiva śatru-vat
"For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
— Bhagavad Gītā 6.6

Is Mind Control Possible?

Lord Kṛṣṇa does not say it is impossible. He agrees it is difficult, but gives a definitive two-step formula to achieve it:

श्रीभगवानुवाच |
असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम् |
अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते ||
śrī-bhagavān uvāca
asaṁśayaṁ mahā-bāho mano durnigrahaṁ calam
abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate
Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa said: "O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice (Abhyāsa) and by detachment (Vairāgya)."
— Bhagavad Gītā 6.35

The Art of Withdrawal

The mind will naturally wander towards sense objects. The practice (Abhyasa) consists of actively witnessing its wanderings and bringing it back to the Supreme.

यतो यतो निश्चरति मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम् |
ततस्ततो नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव वशं नयेत् ||
yato yato niścalati
manaś cañcalam asthiram
tatas tato niyamyaitad
ātmany eva vaśaṁ nayet
"From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
— Bhagavad Gītā 6.26

The Practical Formula (ABCD)

In ISKCON, we practice a very practical methodology for regulating the mind, easily remembered as ABCD:

  • A Association: Keep the association of positive, spiritually-minded devotees (Sādhu-saṅga). The mind absorbs the qualities of those it associates with.
  • B Books: Read Vedic literatures like Bhagavad Gītā and Śrīmad Bhāgavatam daily to infuse the mind with spiritual intelligence.
  • C Chanting: The highest form of Abhyāsa. Mantra literally means "that which delivers the mind" (man - mind, tra - deliberate/deliver).
  • D Diet: Consume only karma-free, sanctified food (Kṛṣṇa Prasādam). What we eat heavily influences the subtle composition of our mind.

The Ultimate Solution

By constantly engaging the mind in the service and remembrance of Lord Kṛṣṇa, the mind naturally becomes pacified. The most powerful anchor for a restless mind in Kali Yuga is the Holy Name.
